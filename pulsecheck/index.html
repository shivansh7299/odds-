      Channel guide

      Where a Muse headband listens

      Seen from above, nose at the top. Every channel is the voltage between its sensor and the reference at Fpz.

      AF7 · AF8 · forehead EEG

      Dry sensors on the forehead above the outer eyebrows, over the frontal lobes. They hear frontal theta during effort or drowsiness, but also every blink and eye movement, because the eye is a small battery right next to them. A blink moves both the same way; looking sideways moves them in opposite directions.

      blinks 50–200 µV · theta 4–8 Hz

      TP9 · TP10 · behind-the-ear EEG

      Rubber sensors behind the ears, over the temporal lobes. They show alpha (8–13 Hz) when the eyes close, and a lot of jaw-muscle noise because the temporalis muscle sits right there. They need skin contact: hair or glasses arms lift them.

      alpha peak ≈ 10 Hz

      PPG · finger pleth

      Light absorption by blood in the fingertip. Each beat gives a systolic peak and a dicrotic notch. Peak-to-peak intervals give heart rate; pulse amplitude shrinks when sympathetic tone constricts finger vessels.

      arbitrary units · peaks marked ●

      RESP · chest belt

      Chest expansion. Heart rate speeds up on inhalation and slows on exhalation: respiratory sinus arrhythmia, a vagal signature that is strongest at rest and with slow, deep breaths.

      8–20 breaths/min at rest

      EDA · skin conductance

      Eccrine sweat gland activity, driven only by sympathetic nerves. A slow tonic level (SCL) plus fast phasic responses (SCRs) that start 1–3 s after a stimulus, peak within a few seconds, and recover slowly.

      SCL 2–20 µS · SCR ≥ 0.05 µS

      TEMP · skin temperature

      Peripheral skin temperature follows blood flow, so it falls slowly under sustained stress as vessels constrict. It changes over tens of seconds, which is why the scope autoscales to hundredths of a degree.

      ≈ 31–35 °C at the finger

      All signals are generated in your browser from simple physiological models sampled at 250 Hz. Amplitudes and time courses are realistic for teaching; they are not recordings and not for clinical use.

      Camera

      Camera off

      Front camera, no contact. Face a window or lamp so your face is evenly lit, hold the phone steady, keep still and don't talk. Every frame is analysed on this device and never stored or sent. Heart rate only: for HRV use a fingertip reading or a chest strap.

      For learning only: this counts beats and shows the two heart sounds. It can't detect murmurs or valve problems, and it isn't a medical device.

      Rear camera with flash works best. iPhone Safari can't turn the flash on from a web page, so hold your finger toward a bright light.

      Garmin Forerunner 265: stream heart rate live
      1. On the watch, hold UP (or open the controls menu) and add Broadcast Heart Rate. Or start any activity and turn on Broadcast During Activity in its settings.
      2. Open this page in Chrome or Edge on Android, Windows, macOS or ChromeOS (not iPhone: iOS browsers have no Web Bluetooth).
      3. Press Connect a watch and pick the Forerunner. Heart rate arrives about once a second; the 3D heart beats at your rate.

      This uses the standard Bluetooth Heart Rate profile, so chest straps (HRM-Pro, Polar H10), Wahoo, Coros and Apple Watch through a broadcast app work the same way. Straps also send beat-to-beat (RR) intervals, which give live HRV; the Forerunner's broadcast usually sends heart rate only.

      Your wearable data
      
      
      Load the export files from your devices to see your own heart, sleep, breathing, temperature and EEG data next to the physiology behind it. Files are read inside this browser tab. Nothing is uploaded or saved, and closing the tab clears it. The only exception is the optional Gemini analysis below, which sends a numeric summary once you add your own API key.

      Apple Watch / Apple Health
        
      1. On iPhone, open Health and tap your profile picture.
      2. Tap Export All Health Data and wait for it to finish.
      3. Save export.zip to Files, then load it here.
      
        
      Reads heart rate samples, resting HR, HRV (SDNN), sleep, steps, breathing rate, blood oxygen and wrist temperature.
      
      
      Garmin
        
      1. Sign in to your Garmin account on the web and open Data Management.
      2. Request Export Your Data; Garmin emails a download link.
      3. Load the .zip, or the .json files inside it.
      
        
      Reads resting HR, steps, sleep stages and respiration where present. FIT activity files are not supported yet. For live heart rate, use Broadcast Heart Rate on the watch and the Live pulse check tab.
      
      
      Oura
        
      1. Sign in to your Oura account on the web.
      2. Find the data export option and download CSV or JSON.
      3. Load the files here.
      
        
      Reads readiness, sleep duration and score, HRV (RMSSD), resting HR, temperature deviation, breathing rate and steps.
      
      
      Muse headband
        
      1. Record a session with the Mind Monitor app.
      2. Turn on raw EEG recording if you want the waveform view.
      3. Export the session CSV and load it here.
      
        
      Reads band powers at TP9, AF7, AF8 and TP10, raw EEG, blinks, jaw clenches and heart rate.
      
      
      Other trackers
        
      1. Export a CSV or JSON from the device's app or website.
      2. It needs a date or timestamp column.
      3. Columns named like heart rate, resting HR, HRV, sleep, steps, temperature, SpO2 or respiration are recognized.
      
        
      Live streaming works only for devices that broadcast standard Bluetooth heart rate (Garmin, Polar, Wahoo, Coros, chest straps), in the Live pulse check tab. Oura and Muse don't broadcast to browsers, so load their exports here.
